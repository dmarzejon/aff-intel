On Nov 9, 2026, Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2, its top models for hard, long-running technical work. It was the top story on Hacker News overnight, and Anthropic's demos (a laptop-bootable operating system built by more than 1,000 coordinated agents over eight days, three working electronics prototypes with Diode, a formal proof of Fermat's Last Theorem in 30 hours) are all over X. For buyers, the questions are simpler: which Claude plan actually lets you use it, what it costs on the API, and when the cheaper Opus 5.5 is the better call. The same day, Meta launched Muse Ember at $0.10/$0.50, and OpenRouter confirmed last week's free stealth model Crouton Alpha was an early Muse Ember. We read Anthropic's model page, API docs, Help Center and Claude Code changelog, plus Meta's and OpenRouter's pages, on the morning of Nov 10.

Quick verdict

You're on Claude Max ($100) or Ultra ($200): Fable 5.2 is already included, up to 50% of your weekly limit. It uses limits faster than other Claude models, so save it for the jobs Opus 5.5 can't finish. You're on Claude Pro ($20): Fable isn't in your plan's limits. You can use it, but only with pay-as-you-go usage credits, and there's no one-time promo credit for 5.2. If you'd use it more than occasionally, Max is the cheaper path. You build on the API: Fable 5.2 is $8 in / $40 out per million tokens, 20% less than Fable 5.1. That's still 2x Opus 5.5 ($4/$20). Batch halves it to $4/$20. You run high-volume agent sub-tasks (routing, extraction, tool calls): don't use Fable. Muse Ember ($0.10/$0.50) or Claude Haiku 5.5 (same headline price) are 80x cheaper per token. You do cyber or life-science research: Mythos 5.2 is the same model with fewer safeguards, open only to organizations admitted to Anthropic's verification programs.

What Anthropic shipped on Nov 9

  • Two names, one model: Fable 5.2 is open to everyone, with safeguards that block risky cybersecurity and biology requests. Mythos 5.2 keeps those capabilities for organizations admitted through Anthropic's Cyber or Life Sciences verification programs
  • Speed: outputs generate up to 30% faster than the 5.1 generation, per Anthropic
  • Context: 1M tokens in, up to 128K out. Model ID claude-fable-5-2

  • Anthropic's own advice: keep using Opus 5.5 for everyday coding and save Fable for the most ambitious open-ended work
  • Benchmarks people are quoting: Fable 5.2 scored 65 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5, the highest so far, per Artificial Analysis as reported on Nov 9–10. Coverage also notes it's slow and expensive per task on that index, so cheaper tokens don't mean cheaper jobs

Muse Ember, and what happened to Crouton Alpha

Meta released Muse Ember on Nov 9 as its smallest, most efficient model, built for the work agents repeat all day: tool calls, coding sub-tasks, routing, extraction and long-document parsing. It's on Meta's Model API as muse-ember-1.0, in Muse Code, and on OpenRouter. OpenRouter's page now says the free stealth model Crouton Alpha (Nov 3) was an early version of Muse Ember and that the stealth period has ended, so the free run is over. The obvious pairing is a big model that plans and a small one that executes: Fable or Opus for the plan, Ember or Haiku for the hundreds of small steps.

Which to pick

  • Solo developer on Claude Pro who hits a wall a few times a month: use usage credits for Fable on those jobs and stay on Pro. If it's weekly, move to Max ($100)
  • Heavy Claude Code user: Max or Ultra. Default to Opus 5.5, switch to Fable with /model for migrations, gnarly debugging and long agent runs, and watch the weekly meter
  • Building an agent product: Fable 5.2 via Batch ($4/$20) for planning, Muse Ember or Haiku 5.5 for execution steps. Cost out a real task; per-token price isn't per-task price
  • Was using Crouton Alpha for free: it's gone. Muse Ember is the paid version at $0.10/$0.50
