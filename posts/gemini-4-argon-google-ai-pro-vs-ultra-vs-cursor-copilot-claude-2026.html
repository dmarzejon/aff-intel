Gemini 4 Argon is out: do you need Google AI Ultra, or is AI Pro, the API, Cursor, Copilot, or Claude Pro enough? (2026)

On Oct 13, 2026, Google rolled out Gemini 4 Argon globally. It's Google's new frontier model, first announced Sep 30 for a small group of cyber defenders. Google's launch posts on X passed 2 million views on @GoogleDeepMind and 3.3 million on @antigravity within a day. The question flooding replies is simple: "Do I have to pay $99.99 for Ultra?" The answer depends on where you want to use Argon. In the Gemini app it's on the $19.99 AI Pro plan. In Google's coding tools, it's Ultra-only. Developers can skip the subscription entirely through the API, Cursor, or GitHub Copilot. We read Google's launch blog, the Gemini app post, the Gemini API docs, the Google One plans page, the Cursor and GitHub announcements, and the Claude and Copilot pricing pages on the morning of Oct 14.

Quick verdict

You want Argon for research, writing, analysis, or chat: Google AI Pro ($19.99/month) is enough. Argon is in the Gemini app model picker and in AI Mode in Search, with 5 TB storage and Gemini in Docs and Sheets. You want Argon inside Google Antigravity (desktop or CLI) or AI Studio: that needs Google AI Ultra, at $99.99/month (5x Pro usage) or $199.99/month (20x). Google's developer docs say Argon is "not available on the Free tier or on the Google AI Pro plan" in AI Studio. You want Argon for coding but not a $100 plan: use it where you already code. Cursor added Argon to its model picker on Oct 13 at $2/$10 per million tokens. GitHub Copilot has it on Pro+ ($39) and up, billed from your monthly AI credits. You mostly want the best $20 coding plan: Claude Pro ($20, or $17/month annually) includes Claude Code with usage built in. Cursor says Argon scores on par with Claude Opus 5.5 on its own benchmark, so Argon isn't a must-switch for coding. On Google AI Plus ($4.99) or Free: no Argon. Plus gets the Flash model.

Where Argon is available (and which plan unlocks it)

  • Gemini app + AI Mode in Search: Google AI Pro and Ultra, rolling out globally from Oct 13 ("if you don't see it yet, check back shortly," per @GeminiApp). The model picker now shows 3.5 Flash-Lite, 3.8 Flash, 3.1 Pro, and 4 Argon. Pro gets "expanded" Argon access and Ultra gets "higher" access
  • Google Antigravity (desktop app + CLI): Ultra only at launch, per Google's launch blog and 9to5Google. One plan description on Google One mentions Argon in Antigravity for Pro, but the launch post and developer docs say Ultra. Don't buy Pro expecting Argon in Antigravity until Google clarifies
  • Google AI Studio / Android Studio (Canary): Ultra subscribers, or the paid API tier. Not Free, not AI Pro
  • Gemini API: the paid tier. The model is gemini-4-argon, with an introductory price of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens through Nov 30, 2026, then $4 / $20. Implicit caching discounts cached input 95% (to $0.10/M, then $0.20/M). Thinking tokens bill as output
  • Gemini for business / enterprise: available in Gemini for business, the API, and Antigravity
  • Third-party tools: Cursor (Oct 13, $2/$10 per million tokens), GitHub Copilot (Pro+, Max, Business, Enterprise, billed at provider list price), and Vercel AI Gateway (google/gemini-4-argon, no markup, routable into Claude Code, Codex, or Cursor)
  • Free programs: 10,000 scientists can apply for Antigravity's highest usage tier free for 6 months. Cyber defenders can apply to the Fairwind Program for Argon without cyber guardrails

What Argon actually changes

  • Long-running work: a unified 1M-token input + output window. One API response can now run past 64K tokens, up to 1M, via background "long decode." Thinking levels are low, medium (the default), and high
  • Google's benchmark claims: 77.9% on DeepSWE v1.1 (long-horizon software engineering), #1 on the Vals Index (finance, coding, legal, tax), and 51.3% on Zapier's AutomationBench. Cursor reports 52.8% on CursorBench at default effort, "on par with Opus 5.5." These are vendor numbers, so test on your own tasks
  • Gemini app updates shipped alongside: Antigravity's agentic coding interface inside the Gemini macOS app, a chat-to-Tasks toggle (previously Spark) that keeps working in the background, and 40+ app connections, including Instacart, OpenTable, Adobe, and Shopify
  • API migration gotchas: explicit context caching isn't supported (only implicit), and temperature, top_p, and top_k are ignored. Google recommends raising max_output_tokens to at least 32,768 and setting client timeouts to 15+ minutes
  • Usage limits are compute-based: Gemini app limits factor in prompt complexity, features, and chat length. They refresh every 5 hours up to a weekly cap, and you can extend them with AI credits. Expect Argon, a large model, to use up your allowance faster than Flash. Google's Logan Kilpatrick called Argon "big model smell," with limited ability to serve it widely

Checklist before you pick

  1. Decide where you'll use Argon. Chat and research only → AI Pro. Antigravity or AI Studio → Ultra. Your existing editor → Cursor or Copilot on per-token billing.
  2. Price the API first if you're light or bursty. At the intro $2/$10 rate, a heavy 100K-token-output session costs about a dollar in output. Many developers will spend less than Ultra's $99.99. The rate doubles on Dec 1, so re-check then.
  3. Already on AI Pro? Open the Gemini app model picker. If Argon isn't there yet, the rollout is still reaching your account. Don't upgrade to Ultra just to get it in the app.
  4. Copilot users: confirm you're on Pro+ or higher. Argon draws down AI credits at list price, so watch your budget alerts (75/90/100%).
  5. Cursor on The X Subscription: set a monthly additional usage limit before you run long Argon agent sessions, because third-party models bill from your first request.
  6. Choosing between Google and Claude for coding? Run the same task in Argon (via the API or Cursor) and in Claude Code on Claude Pro before you pay for a $100 tier on either side.
