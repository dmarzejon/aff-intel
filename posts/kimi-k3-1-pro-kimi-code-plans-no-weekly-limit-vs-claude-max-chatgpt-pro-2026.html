Kimi K3.1 Pro: Kimi Code plans with no weekly limit ($19 to $199) vs Claude Pro/Max vs ChatGPT Plus/Pro, and the Oct 25 quota reset (2026)

Moonshot AI released Kimi K3.1 Pro on Friday, Oct 23 (1:15 PM ET). Its launch post on X passed 3.9 million views. The pitch: a 1M-token context, coding scores Moonshot puts close to Claude Opus 5.5, API pricing at $2 input / $10 output per million tokens, and open weights "by Nov 4, 2026." It landed in the same week many Claude Max users on X said their weekly limits were draining in two or three days. The detail that matters for them: Kimi's new plans have no weekly limit on Kimi Code. The launch also had problems of its own. On Sunday, Oct 25 (4:14 PM ET), Kimi apologized for "rate limits and unnecessary quota consumption" and reset 25% of every subscriber's monthly quota. Below: what each Kimi plan costs, how its limits actually work, how it compares with Claude and ChatGPT, and who should try it. We read Kimi's, Anthropic's, and OpenAI's pages on Oct 26.

Quick verdict

You're on Claude Max and the weekly cap is your problem: Kimi Plus ($19, or $15/month billed annually) is a cheap overflow plan to test. New Kimi plans keep a rolling 5-hour window but drop the weekly limit. There's still a monthly credit pool, so it isn't unlimited. Try it on real work before you cancel anything. You only hit Claude's 5-hour limit: Kimi doesn't fix that. It has a 5-hour window too, and Moonshot doesn't publish how big it is. You build on the API: K3.1 Pro is $2/$10, the same list price as GPT-6.1 Sol and Claude Sonnet 5.5, and half of Opus 5.5 ($4/$20). Its edge is that the price is flat across the full 1M context. Its cache reads cost more (10% of input, vs 5% on Sol and Sonnet). You want the most proven coding agent: Claude Code (Pro $20, Max from $100) or Codex (Plus $20, Pro from $100). On Moonshot's own Terminal-Bench 4.0 chart, Opus 5.5 is still slightly ahead (66.4% vs 65.7%).

What Kimi K3.1 Pro is

  • Model: Moonshot calls it its "most capable 3T-class open-weight model": 2.8T total and 104B active parameters (mixture of experts), multimodal, with a 1M-token context. It's a post-trained version of Kimi K3
  • Where it runs: Kimi on the web and mobile apps, Kimi Work (desktop), Kimi Code (pick it with /model), and the Kimi API. There's also a Highspeed version that Moonshot says is 2–3x faster
  • Open weights: promised "by Nov 4, 2026." They aren't out yet. At 2.8T parameters, running it yourself is a server job, not a laptop one
  • Moonshot's benchmark claims (not independently checked by us): Terminal-Bench 4.0 at 65.7%, vs Claude Opus 5.5 at 66.4% and GPT-6 Astra at 58.2%. BenchCAD at 92.7% with tools, vs Opus 5.5 at 96.2%. Moonshot says it removed cyber-related training data for the open-weight release, which lowered its internal exploit score from 90.5% to 81.0%

How Kimi's limits actually work

  • Rolling 5-hour window: applies to Kimi Code on every plan. Send too much in a short time and you're rate-limited until the window rolls over. Moonshot doesn't publish the size of the window
  • No weekly limit on new plans: Plus, Pro, Max, and Ultra have no weekly cap on Kimi Code. Legacy plans (Moderato, Allegretto, Allegro, Vivace) keep their weekly limit unless you switch. Switching is optional and prorated
  • Monthly shared credit pool: chat, agents, Kimi Code, Kimi Work, and Kimi Claw all draw from one pool that refreshes monthly, with no rollover. If you use it up, Kimi Code freezes too. Kimi's apology says 25% of the monthly quota equals "one week of usage," so treat the pool as roughly four working weeks
  • Hidden drains: a deployed Kimi Claw cloud host costs about 0.6% of your credits every day, and a published agent website about 0.08%. Delete what you don't use
  • After the limit: "Extra Usage" is pay-as-you-go, priced close to the API, with the balance shown in RMB. It's off until you turn it on, and you can set a monthly spending cap
  • Check usage: type /usage in the Kimi Code CLI, or open Settings > Subscription on the web

The Oct 25 apology and quota reset

Kimi's X post (Oct 25, 4:14 PM ET): "We're sorry for the rate limits and unnecessary quota consumption on the K3.1 Pro subscription plan. These were our issues, and we should have fixed them sooner." Every subscriber on every plan gets a one-time reset that restores 25% of their monthly quota. If you've used less than 25%, you go back to zero. Kimi said usage bars would show it "by end of day PST." It also said it is "reducing unnecessary subagent calls and output length, so your quota goes further." Reuters, via CNA, notes Moonshot also had capacity problems after K3 launched. That matters if you're thinking of moving your main workflow over: launch-week capacity is a real risk. Keep your current plan until you've had a smooth week on Kimi.

Kimi cache writes are $2.00 (5-minute TTL) or $4.00 (1-hour TTL) on the standard model. K3.1 Pro "always reasons," and reasoning tokens bill as output. reasoning_effort defaults to max, so set it to low or high for routine calls. The older Kimi K3 is listed at $3/$15.

Worked example (our arithmetic, list prices, cache writes ignored): an agent run with 20M cached input, 2M fresh input, and 1M output costs about $18 on K3.1 Pro (20 × $0.20 + 2 × $2 + 1 × $10), $16 on GPT-6.1 Sol or Sonnet 5.5 if each request stays under Sol's 272K threshold, and $32 on Opus 5.5. With little caching (10M fresh input, 2M output), K3.1 Pro, Sol, and Sonnet are all about $40, and Opus 5.5 is $80. Kimi's cheapest case is long single requests over 272K tokens, where Sol's price goes up and Kimi's doesn't.

Which to pick

  • Claude Max user who runs out mid-week: add Kimi Plus ($19) for a month as overflow before upgrading to Max 20x. Point routine tasks at it and keep Claude for the hard ones
  • Claude Pro user deciding whether to pay $100: a second $19–$20 plan (Kimi Plus or ChatGPT Plus) is a cheaper test than Max 5x
  • Existing Kimi legacy subscriber: if you keep hitting the weekly limit, switching to a new plan removes it. Pricing is unchanged and the switch is prorated
  • API builder with long prompts: price K3.1 Pro against Sol on your real token mix, especially if requests go over 272K. If your traffic is cache-heavy, Sol and Sonnet's cheaper cache reads may win
  • Want to self-host: wait for the weights (promised by Nov 4) and check the license and hardware needs before you plan around it
  • Data-sensitive work: Moonshot is based in Beijing. Its blog says Zero Data Retention is available for API customers. Check your company's rules before sending client code to any provider
