Gemini 4 Neon is out: AI Pro gets it (Carbon stays Ultra), $3/$15 API with no free tier, Copilot and Cursor, and is it a better buy than Carbon after Dec 1, GPT-6.2 Sol/Astra or Claude Opus 5.5? (Nov 2026)

Update, Nov 23: Cheaper way into Neon this week: Google is offering up to 50% off Google AI Plus and annual Google AI Pro to people who already pay for Google AI or Google One storage, through Nov 26 (Google says the exact offer depends on your current plan). At the full 50%, a first year of AI Pro is about $100 instead of $199.99. Ultra, the only plan with Carbon, isn't included. Offers for non-subscribers start on Black Friday, Nov 27. Details and how it compares with ChatGPT Plus and Claude Pro (no Black Friday deals) in our Black Friday AI deals guide.

Update, Nov 20: Another reason to weigh Ultra against Pro: Genie 4, Google's interactive world model, rolled out on Nov 18 to eligible Google AI Ultra subscribers in Project Genie (labs.google/fx/projectgenie), with a creation agent and a community gallery. AI Pro subscribers can only join a waitlist; access is granted gradually. Google says generating worlds there doesn't use your AI credits, but daily limits apply. On Nov 19 Genie 4 became generally available in the Gemini API (paid tier only, no free tier, Batch, Flex or Priority) at $1.50 per million input tokens, $9 per million text output tokens and $17.50 per million video output tokens, which Google puts at about $0.10 per second of 720p, 24 fps video with stereo audio (~$6.05 a minute). Sessions run up to 120 seconds, so one full session is about $12. Billing continues until your code calls complete() or cancel(); closing the tab doesn't stop it.

At about 4:30 PM ET on Wednesday, Nov 18, 2026, Google released Gemini 4 Neon (gemini-4-neon), a model that sits between the fast Hydrogen and the top-end Carbon. Sundar Pichai's launch post passed 1.2 million views overnight, and Logan Kilpatrick's passed 870,000. It landed one day after OpenAI's GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol, so the questions in the replies are about money: which Google plan gets it, what the API costs, whether it's in Copilot and Cursor, and whether it beats Carbon, GPT-6.2 or Claude per dollar. We read Google's announcement, the Gemini API pricing page, Google's subscriptions page and Help Center, GitHub's changelog, Cursor's and OpenRouter's model pages and Artificial Analysis's model pages on the morning of Nov 19.

Quick verdict

You pay for Google AI Pro ($19.99/month): this is the news. Neon is in the Gemini app for AI Pro, not just Ultra, and replaces Argon in the "Deeper analysis" slot. Carbon is still Ultra-only. For most Pro subscribers, Neon is now the best model they can pick. You use Carbon on the API: Carbon's $2/$10 intro price ends Nov 30 and goes to $4/$20. Neon is $3/$15, 25% less per token from Dec 1, and Artificial Analysis scores it about the same (61 vs 60) for $6.44 vs $7.76 per index task in 21.5 vs 37.1 minutes. Plan to switch defaults before Dec 1. You want the cheapest good API model: Neon isn't it. On Artificial Analysis, GPT-6.2 Sol scores 57 for $1.01 per task and GPT-6.2 Astra 62 for $4.00. Neon at its top setting writes a lot of tokens (283M on the index, over four times the median). You want Claude-level coding for less: Neon lists 25% below Opus 5.5 ($4/$20), scores 61 vs 59 and cost $6.44 vs $10.04 per task. Google's own FrontierCode 2.0 numbers put Opus slightly ahead (47.3% vs 46.2%). You code in an editor: it's in GitHub Copilot Pro+, Max, Business and Enterprise at list price, and in Cursor as a third-party model billed outside included usage. Unlike GPT-6.2, you can use it in both.

  • The model: Google calls it "frontier-level intelligence with fast end-to-end task completion" for software engineering, knowledge work and science. 1M-token context, long output of up to 1M tokens through continuation, text/image/audio/video in, text out, and four thinking levels: Low, Medium, High (default) and Extra High
  • Where: the Gemini API (paid tier) in Google AI Studio and Android Studio; Gemini for business (API and model dropdown); Google Antigravity for paid AI Pro and Ultra subscribers; the Gemini app for AI Pro and Ultra subscribers. Also GitHub Copilot, Cursor, OpenRouter and Vercel's AI Gateway on day one
  • Gemini app picker now: Auto (Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite for paid users, per 9to5Google), Everyday help = 4 Hydrogen, Deeper analysis = 4 Neon, Code & complex work = 4 Carbon. Argon and Gemini 3.1 Pro are gone from the app picker. Tasks in Gemini can now run on Neon
  • Cyber variant: "Gemini 4 Neon for cyber" goes to eligible members of Google's Fairwind Program over the next few days, through Cloud account teams
  • Vendor benchmarks (Google-reported): #2 on the Vals Index (70.9%), #1 on Artificial Analysis's Coding Agent Index (58.6), 65.2% on Terminal-Bench 4.1, 46.2% on FrontierCode 2.0 at High, 56.3% on Zapier's AutomationBench, and 12.8% on SWE-sweep with the mini-SWE harness vs 39.0% inside Antigravity. Google also reports its lowest fabrication rate yet in internal Antigravity testing

What a request costs (list prices)

One call with 100K input and 10K output tokens, no caching: Gemini 4 Neon $0.45 · Carbon $0.30 now, $0.60 from Dec 1 · Hydrogen $0.225 · GPT-6.2 Sol $0.30 · Claude Sonnet 5.5 $0.30 · Claude Opus 5.5 $0.60 · GPT-6.2 Astra $1.20. VentureBeat's rule of thumb: Neon has to use about a third fewer tokens than Sol or Sonnet to match them per job. On Artificial Analysis it used more, not fewer, at Extra High; at High it scored 60 with 123M output tokens instead of 283M, so High is the setting to test first.

  • Batch: $1.50 / $7.50 ($0.075 cached), half price, on Google's pricing page
  • Flex and Priority: OpenRouter lists a Google AI Studio Flex endpoint at $1.50 / $7.50 and Priority endpoints at $5.40 / $27
  • Caching: implicit caching only; Google says explicit caching isn't supported for Neon
  • Search grounding: 5,000 free requests a month shared across Gemini 3 and newer models, then $14 per 1,000
  • No free tier: Google's pricing page says Neon "is not available on the Free tier", and thinking tokens are billed as output. AI Pro and Ultra subscribers can try it in AI Studio

Google plans: Free vs AI Pro vs Ultra

  • Free / AI Plus: no Neon in the Gemini app. Google's subscriptions page lists Neon from AI Pro up
  • AI Pro ($19.99/month in the US): Neon and Hydrogen in the Gemini app, 4x standard usage limits, 1M-token context, plus Neon in Google Antigravity. Google doesn't publish a Neon message count; limits are compute-based, refresh every 5 hours and stop at a weekly cap, and you can buy AI credits to extend them
  • AI Ultra ($99.99 for 5x Pro limits, $199.99 for 20x): adds Carbon and "highest access" to Neon and Hydrogen. As of Nov 18, both Ultra tiers also include YouTube Premium Family for up to five household members
  • Who should upgrade: if you were paying for Ultra only to get a stronger model than Argon, try Neon on Pro first. Artificial Analysis puts it level with Carbon on its index

Coding tools: Antigravity, Copilot, Cursor, OpenRouter

  • Google Antigravity: paid AI Pro and Ultra. Google pitches Neon as an orchestrator directing parallel Hydrogen subagents (its /swarm demos), and its own SWE-sweep numbers triple inside Antigravity
  • GitHub Copilot: generally available Nov 18 for Pro+, Max, Business and Enterprise (not plain Pro), rolling out gradually in VS Code, Visual Studio, JetBrains, Xcode, Eclipse, Copilot CLI, the coding agent, the Copilot app, github.com and mobile. Billed at $3 / $0.15 / $15 per million under usage-based billing. Business and Enterprise admins control it through model policy; new models are on by default
  • Cursor: Neon has a Cursor model page. It's a third-party model, so it isn't part of the usage included with Cursor plans; it's billed from prepaid credit on Cursor's current subscription or the "Other Models" pool on legacy plans. Unlike GPT-6.2, which OpenAI isn't supplying to Cursor, Google's new models are there
  • OpenRouter: google/gemini-4-neon at $3 / $15 via Google Vertex and AI Studio, with EU in-region routing on Vertex at $3.30 / $16.50 for Business and Enterprise accounts

Caveats before you switch

  • Slow to start at Extra High: Artificial Analysis measured 56.5 seconds to the first answer token and 110 tokens/second after that (High: 40.6 s). "Fast" means fast to finish compared with Carbon, not fast to reply
  • Verbose: 283M output tokens on the index at Extra High vs a 65.1M median for its price class. That's why its per-task cost is above Astra's despite cheaper tokens
  • Rank moves: Google's post says #3 on Artificial Analysis; by Nov 19 the model page showed #6 of 96 as more models were added. Its score (61) didn't change
  • Most headline benchmarks are Google's. We label them as vendor-reported. Test on your own repo or documents

Which to pick

  • Google AI Pro subscriber: switch your default to Neon for research, documents and harder chats. Keep Hydrogen for quick questions to save usage
  • Considering Ultra for Carbon: try Neon on Pro for a week first. Ultra still makes sense for Carbon, Deep Think, the higher caps and, now, YouTube Premium Family
  • Carbon or Argon on the API: benchmark Neon at High now; from Dec 1 it's 25% cheaper per token than either. See our Carbon guide
  • Lowest cost per finished task: GPT-6.2 Sol, then Hydrogen. See our GPT-6.2 guide and Hydrogen guide
  • Opus 5.5 user on a budget: Neon is the closest like-for-like swap at 25% lower list price. Keep Opus where it wins on your tests
  • Cursor user: Neon (and Carbon) work in Cursor; GPT-6.2 doesn't. Copilot Pro+ gets all three
