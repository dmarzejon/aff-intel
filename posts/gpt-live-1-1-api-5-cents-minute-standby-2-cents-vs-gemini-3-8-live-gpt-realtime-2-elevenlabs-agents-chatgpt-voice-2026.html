Quick verdict

You're building a voice agent on OpenAI: move from GPT-Live-1 to GPT-Live-1.1 now. Same price ($0.05 per active minute), better transcription of letters and numbers and steadier accents across languages. It's a one-line model change. Your calls have long silences (hold music, a user reading, a kiosk waiting): turn on standby. Idle minutes drop from $0.05 to $0.02, 60% less, and the WebRTC call and conversation history stay open. It works on GPT-Live-1 too. You want the lowest voice bill: Google's Gemini 3.8 Live is priced per audio minute at $0.005 in and $0.018 out and has a free tier. It's likely cheaper per call, but it bills tokens, not wall-clock time, so meter a test call before comparing. You want a no-code agent platform: ElevenLabs Agents is $0.08 a minute plus the LLM, with telephony through your own provider and a 95% discount on silences over 10 seconds. You pay more per minute for the builder, voices and phone tooling. You just use ChatGPT Voice: nothing to buy. What launched

  • The model: GPT-Live-1.1 "can listen while it speaks". OpenAI says that compared with GPT-Live-1 it transcribes speech more accurately and keeps a more consistent accent and voice across languages, and handles "precise details like letters and numbers". Audio and text in, audio and text out; streaming and function calling
  • How it works: GPT-Live handles the conversation and hands harder requests to a backend model you choose (Responses delegation to an OpenAI model, or client delegation to your own). You describe in plain language when it should delegate
  • Where: the Live API only (v1/live/sessions). It doesn't work on the older Realtime API, Chat Completions or Responses. GPT-Live-1.1 and GPT-Live-1 have separate concurrent-session limits
  • Standby (new, both GPT-Live models): set session.wake_policy.type to speech and an idle timeout of 5 to 60 seconds. After that much silence the model pauses, the WebRTC connection and session ID stay the same, and it resumes with history when someone speaks. WebRTC only. The first reply after standby can take longer
  • Also available: Vercel's AI Gateway listed openai/gpt-live-1.1 on launch day at the same per-second rate. It isn't on OpenRouter, which doesn't list any GPT-Live model. Copilot and Cursor don't apply: this is a voice API, not a coding model

What a call costs

Take a 10-minute support call with 3 minutes of hold or silence, before backend model costs:

  • GPT-Live-1.1 without standby: 10 × $0.05 = $0.50
  • GPT-Live-1.1 with standby (assuming all 3 quiet minutes land on standby after the timeout): 7 × $0.05 + 3 × $0.02 = $0.41. Short pauses under your timeout still bill as active
  • ElevenLabs Agents: 10 × $0.08 = $0.80 before the silence discount; with 3 minutes of long silences at 95% off, about $0.57, plus the LLM. Telephony is billed by your carrier
  • Gemini 3.8 Live: depends on how many audio minutes Google counts in and out. If the model speaks for 4 minutes, output audio alone is 4 × $0.018 = $0.072; input audio is $0.005 per counted minute. We don't publish a total because Google bills tokens, and context carried between turns may count. Run a metered test call

Two OpenAI billing details from its cost guide: creating a WebRTC session bills 15 seconds up front, which is credited against the call once it runs (it matters if you create sessions before users speak), and muting the mic doesn't trigger standby; only the idle timeout does. Backend calls, for example to GPT-6.2 Sol at $2/$10 per million tokens, are extra on every OpenAI option.

ChatGPT Voice: Free vs Plus vs Pro

  • Plus ($20/month) and up: GPT-Live-1.1 has been the default voice model since Oct 15, including in Codex and dots, per OpenAI's system card. The API launch doesn't change your plan
  • Free and other users: get GPT-Live-1.1 Quip, a smaller version, as the default
  • Dec 11: OpenAI retires Advanced and Standard voice. Live video and screen sharing stay on Advanced voice until GPT-Live supports them

Caveats before you switch

  • Different API: if you're on the Realtime API with GPT-Realtime-2, GPT-Live is a migration, not a model swap. OpenAI has a migration guide
  • Standby is documented for WebRTC only. OpenAI's pricing and docs describe the $0.02 rate for WebRTC sessions; if you connect over WebSocket or SIP, check before counting on it
  • Quality claims are OpenAI's. Its system card says the API model scores higher than GPT-Live-1 on mental-health, emotional-reliance and self-harm safety evaluations and at parity elsewhere; there's no independent voice benchmark yet. Early user posts on X compare its naturalness favorably with ElevenLabs, but that's opinion
  • Per-minute isn't per-call. The voice line is often the smaller part of the bill once a backend model does tool calls. Price both

Which to pick

  • Already on GPT-Live-1: switch the model ID to gpt-live-1.1 and add standby with a 15 to 30 second timeout. No price change
  • Phone or kiosk agent with long waits: GPT-Live with standby, or ElevenLabs if you need its no-code builder and telephony integrations
  • Cost-first, high volume: start with Gemini 3.8 Live's free tier, then compare a metered paid call with GPT-Live at $0.05/min
  • Need reasoning inside the voice model itself: GPT-Realtime-2 (configurable reasoning) or Gemini 3.8 Live Extended Thinking
  • Consumer: ChatGPT Plus already includes GPT-Live-1.1. See our GPT-6.2 guide for what else Plus gets
