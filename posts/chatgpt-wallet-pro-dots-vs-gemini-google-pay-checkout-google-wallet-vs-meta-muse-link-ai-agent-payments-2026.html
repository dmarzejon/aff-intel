ChatGPT Wallet vs Gemini (Google Pay checkout + Google Wallet) vs Meta Muse: which AI agent can actually pay for you, what plan you need, and what it can see (Nov 2026)

On Friday, Nov 13, 2026, two "wallet" launches landed a few hours apart. OpenAI introduced ChatGPT Wallet, which lets your ChatGPT dot (OpenAI's always-on agent) pay at checkout with a tokenized card after you approve each purchase with Face ID or a fingerprint. Google connected Google Wallet to the Gemini app for every US user, so Gemini can read your spending (with banks and cards linked through Plaid), but Google's help page says it can't make transactions with the payment methods in Wallet. Same word, very different products. Meanwhile Meta's Muse agent has been checking out for people since early October. The questions buyers are asking: which one can actually buy something for me, what does it cost to get access, and what do the agent and the store get to see? We read OpenAI's launch post and Wallet help article, OpenAI's Pro tiers page, Google's blog and two Gemini help pages, Plaid's partner post, Meta's Muse payments help page, CNBC, The Verge and 9to5Google on the morning of Nov 16.

Quick verdict

You want an AI agent to complete a purchase with your own card details hidden: ChatGPT Wallet is the most locked-down design so far: a network payment token per purchase, a biometric check every time, and your card's rewards and dispute protections kept. The catch is access: it's ChatGPT Pro only (Pro 100 is $100/month), US, 18+, eligible US-issued credit cards (Amex, Chase, Mastercard, Visa), no debit cards, and it's rolling out gradually. You don't want to pay $100 a month to try agent checkout: Gemini's shopping feature has a Buy button for select merchants on gemini.google.com that pays with Google Pay, and Meta Muse can check out through Link by Stripe (one-time card number). Neither needs a ChatGPT-style premium plan for checkout per their help pages. You want spending insights, not purchases: the new Google Wallet connector in Gemini is open to all US Gemini app users, read-only. ChatGPT Finances (US Free through Pro) and Claude Personal Finance (US Pro, Max, Ultra) do the same job; see our Claude vs ChatGPT finance comparison. You use Claude: Claude has no purchase wallet today. Personal Finance is read-only and can't move money.

What launched on Nov 13

  • ChatGPT Wallet (OpenAI): built with American Express, Mastercard and Visa. You add a card once; before each purchase your dot shows the merchant, items, total, card and any shipping or recurring-payment terms; you approve with biometrics; the payment network issues a token (its own card number, security code and expiry), so neither the agent nor the store sees your real card number. OpenAI calls it "the first wallet that lets agents pay without access to users' actual card details and that requires a security check for every purchase"
  • Google Wallet in Gemini (Google): a new connected app under Personal Intelligence. Tag @Google Wallet to chart spending, find subscriptions that went up in price, or set a holiday budget from last year's transactions. You can link bank, card, loan and investment accounts to Wallet through Plaid. Google: Gemini "can only read information from your accounts; it can't move, manage, or invest your money"
  • Already live: Gemini's shopping feature (Buy button on eligible listings, Pay with GPay) and Meta Muse checkout (approval card, Link by Stripe or a saved login)

What each one can see

  • ChatGPT Wallet: your card is entered in a secure form, "not in chat," and the business gets a token, not your number. The merchant may still need your name, phone, email and address, which you may be asked for in the conversation. Cards saved in Wallet are separate from your ChatGPT subscription card
  • Gemini + Google Wallet: transaction data from accounts you link through Plaid, plus the cards and passes in Wallet (for rewards and offers). You can switch the connection off in Personal Intelligence settings and manage Plaid links in Plaid Portal. Google also added a Memory Summary page (Profile → Personal Intelligence) to see and edit what Gemini remembers
  • Meta Muse: if you let Muse log in for you, it uses credentials stored in Meta's Secure Credential Store and you can watch the site as Muse sees it before any transaction. Meta's help page: "You're responsible for all transactions your Muse makes on your behalf"

Before you let any agent buy for you

  • Read the approval screen like a receipt: merchant, total, card, and especially recurring-payment terms. OpenAI warns that if you agree to a subscription, the merchant may store the credential and charge it again
  • "Approved" isn't "ordered": OpenAI's help says "Ready to check out" doesn't confirm the merchant placed your order. Check for the merchant's confirmation before retrying, or you may buy twice. Google's help gives the same advice for "Something went wrong"
  • Use a credit card, not debit: ChatGPT Wallet requires one anyway, and credit cards carry the strongest dispute rights
  • Prefer one-time card numbers over saved logins: on Muse, that means Link by Stripe rather than letting the agent sign in to your store accounts
  • No spending limits yet: OpenAI says limits by amount, category or time period are coming "over time." For now every purchase needs your approval, which is the safeguard

Which to pick

  • Already on ChatGPT Pro for Codex: you already have a dot; add a credit card to Wallet and try a small purchase first. Don't upgrade to Pro just for Wallet
  • On ChatGPT Plus or Go: personal dots are Pro-only, so no Wallet. Use ChatGPT for research and comparison, and buy yourself
  • Google user who wants budgeting help for free: connect Google Wallet in Gemini (gemini.google.com/customize/connectors), link accounts through Plaid only if you're comfortable, and ask for a subscription audit. It can't spend anything
  • Want agent checkout without a premium plan: try Gemini's Buy button on eligible listings or Muse with Link by Stripe
  • Claude user: stick with Personal Finance (Pro, Max, Ultra in the US) for read-only insights; there's no Claude checkout to compare yet
