Gemini 4 Hydrogen is out: free in the Gemini app (only through Auto), AI Plus, or the $1.50/$7.50 API, and is it a better buy than Claude Sonnet 5.5 or GPT-6.1 Terra? (Nov 2026)

On Nov 12, 2026, Google released Gemini 4 Hydrogen (gemini-4-hydrogen), the fast, cheaper model in the Gemini 4 family and the replacement for Gemini 3.8 Flash. It's Google's third new model in six weeks, after Argon and Carbon, and the first to drop the "Flash" name. Google AI Studio's launch post passed 95,000 views and 3,100 likes on X in its first day, and Artificial Analysis's benchmark post passed 280,000 views. The questions in the replies: can free users get it, what does it cost on the API now that it's no longer a "Flash" price, and does it beat Claude Sonnet 5.5 or OpenAI's GPT-6.1 Terra for the money? We read Google's launch blog, the Gemini API "what's new" and pricing pages, 9to5Google, Notebookcheck, Artificial Analysis, Cursor's and GitHub's model docs, OpenRouter, and Anthropic's and OpenAI's pricing pages on the morning of Nov 13.

Quick verdict

You use free Gemini: you can't pick Hydrogen. Google says free users get it through Auto model selection "when it's the appropriate model for the prompt"; most prompts still go to Flash-Lite, and with Auto off every reply is Flash-Lite. Keep Auto on. You're on Google AI Plus ($4.99/month): Hydrogen is now the headline model on Google's plans page, with 2x the free tier's limits. Plus subscribers were told they lose the Pro models on Nov 13 (today), so Hydrogen is what you get. AI Pro ($19.99) adds Argon and Hydrogen in Antigravity and Google Sheets. You build on the API: Hydrogen is $1.50 in / $7.50 out per million tokens, flat across the full 1M context, with a free tier. That's twice what Gemini 3.8 Flash costs until Dec 31 ($0.75/$3.75). From Jan 1, 2027, 3.8 Flash rises to the same $1.50/$7.50, so Hydrogen is the default for new work and 3.8 Flash only saves money for the next seven weeks. You're choosing between Hydrogen and Claude or OpenAI: per token, Hydrogen is 25% cheaper than Claude Sonnet 5.5 ($2/$10) and 1.5x GPT-6.1 Terra ($1/$5). Artificial Analysis scores Hydrogen 54 at xhigh, one point under Sonnet 5.5 and GPT-6.1 Sol (55), at about a third of Sonnet's cost per task but about 2.5x Sol's. You code in Cursor or Copilot: Hydrogen is in Cursor as a third-party model billed at Google's rates, not from included usage. GitHub Copilot doesn't list it yet (3.8 Flash, Argon and Carbon only, as of this morning).

What Google shipped on Nov 12

  • Who it's for: Google calls Hydrogen its model for "rapid execution across diverse agentic workflows": everyday coding, tool-using agents, web apps and document work. Carbon and Argon stay the picks for "deep, long-horizon reasoning"
  • Specs: 1,048,576-token window shared by input, thinking and output; up to 65,536 output tokens per request, and up to 1M with background execution. Four thinking levels: low, medium, high (default) and a new xhigh. Carbon and Argon default to medium and stop at high
  • Google's claims vs 3.8 Flash: finishes tasks 2.5x faster at the default setting, more than triples Terminal-Bench 4.1 (57% vs 18.2%), and cuts fabricated claims by more than 5x. Partners saw it run 2x to 2.8x faster with 12% to 23% fewer tokens. These are Google's numbers
  • Antigravity Orchestrator: a new mode that switches between Carbon and Hydrogen in one session. Google says it reached near-Carbon success on Terminal-Bench 4.0 while using 24% less quota and finishing 19% faster on average
  • Search: 9to5Google reports Hydrogen is now the default model for AI Mode and handles "your hardest questions" in AI Overviews, for all users
  • API gotchas when migrating from 3.8 Flash: no explicit context caching (implicit only, 95% off cached input on prompts of 4,096+ tokens); temperature, top_p and top_k are ignored; minimal thinking returns an error; every token is billed, including thinking tokens

Long documents flip the order. Because OpenAI raises Terra's rates above 272K input tokens, one request with a 600,000-token input and an 8,000-token answer costs about $0.96 on Hydrogen, $1.26 on Terra and $1.28 on Sonnet 5.5 (and $0.48 on 3.8 Flash until Dec 31). Under 272K, Terra is the cheapest of the three per token.

  • Against Claude Sonnet 5.5 (55): AA says Hydrogen at xhigh is one point lower at about a third of Sonnet's cost per task (about 32%, per OfficeChai's write-up of AA's data)
  • Against GPT-6.1 Sol (55): Hydrogen at xhigh costs about 2.5x as much per task. Sol is the per-task value pick if you're OK on OpenAI
  • Against GPT-6.1 Terra (51) and Gemini 4 Argon (50): Hydrogen at its default high ties Terra and edges Argon. OfficeChai reports AA puts it at about 19% of Argon's cost per task at high
  • Where it's weaker: terminal work. AA has Hydrogen at 54% on Terminal-Bench 4.0, against 64% for Sonnet 5.5 and 56% for Sol (all at max settings)
  • xhigh is a tax: it nearly doubles output tokens for three points. Start on high, as both Google and Cursor recommend, and use xhigh only for hard debugging

Which to pick

  • Free Gemini user: keep Auto on and check which model answered (on gemini.google.com: the menu under a reply → Show thinking steps → scroll to the bottom). If you need Hydrogen every time, AI Plus at $4.99 is the cheapest way to pick it
  • AI Plus user who lost Pro today: try Hydrogen for a week before you upgrade. Upgrade to AI Pro ($19.99) only if you need Argon, Antigravity or Sheets
  • Developer on 3.8 Flash: stay on 3.8 Flash for cost-sensitive jobs until Dec 31 while it's half price, test Hydrogen on high now, and move before Jan 1, when the prices match
  • Claude Sonnet 5.5 user: Hydrogen is a cheaper second model for long documents and parallel subagents, but Sonnet still leads on terminal and agent coding benchmarks
  • OpenAI user: Terra is cheaper per token under 272K and Sol is cheaper per task. Hydrogen wins on very long prompts, where Terra's rates go up above 272K tokens
  • Copilot user: wait for GitHub to add it, or use it now through Cursor or OpenRouter at Google's rates
