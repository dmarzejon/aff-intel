OpenAI released GPT-6.1 Terra on Monday, Oct 26 (2:06 PM ET). Its launch post on X passed 3.9 million views overnight. Quick verdict

You run high-volume agents that read screens, forms, or documents: test Terra first. It costs half of Sol or Sonnet 5.5 on every line of the price sheet, and it supports the computer_use tool in the Responses API. For very simple extraction, also try GPT-6 Luna or Claude Haiku 5.5. Both are 10x cheaper again ($0.10/$0.50). You're on ChatGPT Plus ($20) and run out of Codex usage: switch routine tasks to Terra. OpenAI's own estimate is 35–285 local messages per 5 hours on Terra vs 15–160 on GPT-6.1 Sol. Your overall plan limit doesn't change; each task just uses less of it. You do hard coding or long refactors: stay on GPT-6.1 Sol, GPT-6 Astra, or Claude Opus 5.5. OpenAI says so itself: "For the most complex coding tasks, GPT-6.1 Sol and GPT-6 Astra remain the better choice." Terra scores 50.3% on Terminal-Bench 4.0 at max effort; Moonshot's chart has Opus 5.5 at 66.4%. You're choosing a $20 plan: Terra is a reason to pick ChatGPT Plus if your work is browser and desktop tasks. For coding-first work, Claude Pro ($20) and Kimi Plus ($19) are still in the running.

What GPT-6.1 Terra is

  • Model: OpenAI calls it "a fast, efficient model for visual reasoning, precise computer use, and clear, natural writing." It replaces GPT-5.6 Terra in the middle of OpenAI's lineup, between GPT-6 Luna (cheapest) and GPT-6.1 Sol / GPT-6 Astra (strongest)
  • Specs (API): model ID gpt-6.1-terra. Text and image input, text output. 1,050,000-token context window (922,000 max input), 128,000 max output, June 1, 2026 knowledge cutoff. Reasoning effort goes from none to max, with medium as the default. Tool calling requires the Responses API
  • Where it runs: the OpenAI API, ChatGPT Work (web, desktop, and mobile), and Codex. Pick it from the model list under Advanced. Microsoft also made it generally available in Foundry on Oct 26
  • OpenAI's benchmark claims (not independently checked by us): on Agents' Last Exam, Terra performs "on par with GPT-6 Astra at only one-tenth its price per token" and finishes tasks "over three times faster" than Astra and Sol. At max effort, OpenAI says it's "over three times as token efficient as Claude Opus 5.5" for "a ~9x lower cost per task." Terminal-Bench 4.0 at max effort: 50.3%, up from 21.5% for GPT-5.6 Terra. OpenAI's internal error rate at max effort fell to 4.3%
  • Safety note: OpenAI says Terra "meets the Critical threshold in cybersecurity" under its Preparedness Framework and keeps the safeguards built for GPT-6 Astra

Terra's cache writes are $1.25 (30-minute retention) or $2.50 (12-hour retention), and cache reads cost 5% of input. Batch and Flex are 50% off ($0.50 / $2.50). Fast mode is 2x ($2 / $10). Token counts aren't directly comparable across providers: Anthropic says Claude 4.7 and later models use a tokenizer that produces about 30% more tokens for the same text, and reasoning tokens bill as output on all of these models.

Which to pick

  • API builder on GPT-6.1 Sol for routine work: A/B Terra on your real traffic. If quality holds, your bill halves with a one-line model change
  • API builder on Claude Sonnet 5.5: Terra is half the list price. Anthropic's cheaper option is Haiku 5.5 at $0.10/$0.50 for prompts up to 100K tokens
  • Computer-use or document agents at volume: Terra is built for this. Also use Batch (50% off) for anything that can wait
  • ChatGPT Plus user who keeps hitting Codex limits: make Terra your default for simple edits, browser tasks, and writing, and save Sol for hard problems
  • Hard coding, big refactors: Sol, Astra, or Opus 5.5. Terra isn't OpenAI's coding pick
  • Long single prompts over 272K tokens: Terra's price goes up (2x input, 1.5x output). Claude Sonnet 5.5 and Kimi K3.1 Pro stay flat across 1M, so price your actual mix
