Cursor is now part of The X Subscription: should you switch, keep your old Cursor plan, or buy Claude Pro or Copilot instead? (2026)

On Oct 12, 2026, SpaceXSI launched The X Subscription, one plan that bundles Grok, Grok Bot, Cursor, and X Premium. It is now the only way for new customers to buy Cursor: the previous Cursor plans are closed to new sign-ups. Paid tiers are Plus $8, Premium $30, Super $100, and Ultra $200 a month. Each includes a weekly usage pool for Grok models that’s shared between Grok and Cursor. The launch post on X passed 2.8 million views overnight. The catch that has Cursor’s forum busy: on the new plans, Claude, GPT, and other third-party models in Cursor aren’t included at all. You pay API list prices for them from the first request. We read the announcement, the pricing pages, the X Subscription help docs, Cursor’s own model pricing page, and the plan pages for Claude and GitHub Copilot on the morning of Oct 13.

Quick verdict

You pay for Cursor Pro, Pro Plus, or Ultra today and mostly use Claude or GPT: don’t switch yet. Your current plan, price, and included third-party usage continue, and a switch is permanent. Support can’t undo it. You mostly code with Grok or Composer in Cursor: Premium ($30) is good value. It bundles Cursor Pro-level features, SuperGrok, Grok Bot, and X Premium+ (previously $90/month combined), with at least twice the Grok coding usage of the comparable old Cursor plan. You’re new and want Claude models for coding: Claude Pro ($20/month) includes Claude Code, so your Claude usage comes with the plan instead of a pay-as-you-go top-up. You want the cheapest editor-based assistant: GitHub Copilot Pro is $10/month with monthly AI credits that work across Copilot’s chat, agent mode, and CLI. You mainly want an X checkmark and some AI: Plus ($8) gets you the checkmark and fewer ads, but in Cursor it only runs Grok models, with no cloud agents or Bugbot.

What changed on Oct 12

  • One plan, four products: Grok (chat, voice, image and video), Grok Bot (persistent AI agents), Cursor (local and cloud coding agents), and X benefits (checkmark, creator tools, fewer ads). Available to new customers from Oct 12 and rolling out globally “over the next few days”
  • Plans: Free $0 (limited Grok, a starter allowance for Cursor’s agents), Plus $8, Premium $30, Super $100, Ultra $200 a month. Included usage is “Base” on Plus, then 4x, 15x, and 50x Plus. Annual billing is $80, $300, $1,000, and $2,000 a year
  • One weekly pool: Grok usage in the Grok apps, the Grok tab on X, Grok Bot, and Cursor all draw from the same pool, and it refreshes weekly instead of monthly. Past it, you can keep going with paid top-ups or wait for the refresh
  • Third-party models: Claude, GPT, and other non-Grok models stay available in Cursor on Premium and above. They aren’t part of the pool, so they’re paid from prepaid credit from the first use, at API list prices. Plus runs Grok models only
  • What Premium adds over Plus: Grok Bot, the latest Grok models, Cursor cloud agents and automations, code hosting via Origin, AI code reviews via Bugbot, and access to third-party models. Super adds priority model access at peak times, and Ultra adds earliest access to new products and models
  • Old plans: existing Cursor, SuperGrok, and X Premium subscribers keep their plan, price, and features. They can switch for a prorated refund on the old plan, plus limited-time offers shown in billing settings. SpaceXSI says it will eventually move remaining customers to the new plans, with no timeline yet and advance notice promised
  • Teams: $30, $100, or $200 per seat, each seat with its own weekly pool. Teams also pay a Cursor Token Rate of $0.25 per million tokens on third-party model requests, on top of API pricing. Individual plans don’t pay it

The fine print before you switch

  • It’s one-way: X’s help docs say a completed switch is permanent and Support can’t undo it. Your old Cursor and SuperGrok plans end with a prorated refund, sent back separately from the new charge
  • Your Claude/GPT allowance disappears: Cursor Pro, Pro Plus, and Ultra each include two pools, one for Cursor’s own models and one for “Other Models.” Cursor says that third-party allowance continues until you switch. After the switch, every Claude or GPT request comes out of prepaid credit
  • What list price means in practice: Cursor’s model page lists Claude Sonnet 5.5 at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, and Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-5.6 Sol at $4 / $20. Cursor’s own guidance says daily agent users typically use $60–$100 a month in total and power users $200+
  • Set a cap first: Auto Top-up stays off unless you turn it on. A monthly “additional usage limit” is the only hard stop. Spend alerts warn you but don’t stop usage. Manage all of it at grok.com/dashboard
  • Grok Bot shares the pool: on Premium and up, Grok Bot draws from the same weekly pool as your Cursor coding, not a separate quota. One early switcher on Cursor’s forum says that makes the pool feel smaller
  • Paying through Apple? Switching on the web does not cancel a Cursor subscription billed through the App Store. Cancel it in iPhone Settings, or Apple keeps charging you
  • Not ready to switch? Existing customers can still move between their current Cursor plans (for example, Pro Plus down to Pro) without switching. App Store plans are the exception

Checklist before you pick

  1. Look at last month’s Cursor usage dashboard. If most of your spend is Claude or GPT, the new plans charge for all of it on top of the subscription. Keeping your current plan is the cheaper move.
  2. Try Grok models on a real task first. If Grok or Composer handles your everyday work, Premium’s larger Grok pool, plus SuperGrok and X Premium+, is likely the better deal than the old $20 Pro.
  3. Check your switch offer. Switching comes with limited-time offers in your billing settings. Read it, but remember the switch can’t be reversed.
  4. Before your first Claude request on a new plan, set a monthly additional usage limit at grok.com/dashboard. Leave Auto Top-up off until you know your burn rate.
  5. On a team? Count the $0.25 per million token Cursor Token Rate on third-party models. Compare that with the lower $30 per-seat price (old Teams Standard seats were $40).
  6. Brand-new and Claude-first? Price Claude Pro ($20, with Claude Code) against Premium ($30) plus your expected Claude top-ups before you commit.
