SWE-2.1 is free in Devin until Dec 8: is Cognition's new coding model worth a $20 Devin Pro plan vs Claude Opus 5.5, GPT-6.2 Sol, Gemini 4 Neon, Kimi K3.1 Pro, Cursor or Copilot's MAI Code 2? (Nov 2026)

On Tuesday, Nov 24, 2026 (about 2 PM ET), Cognition released SWE-2.1, the in-house model behind its Devin coding agent. Cognition's launch post passed 1 million impressions within hours: "Like its predecessor, SWE-2.1 is trained with pure reinforcement learning on Kimi K3... SWE-2.1 is free for all Devin Pro, Max, and Teams for the next two weeks." Alongside it came SWE-2.1 Ultrafast (Cognition's X thread calls it Lightning), served by Cerebras at about 10× the speed, and SWE-2.1 Cyber, a version with looser safeguards for Devin Security customers. We read Cognition's announcement, Devin's models and pricing docs, Devin's plans page and the launch thread on the morning of Nov 25. The model isn't on OpenRouter or any other API we checked: you get it inside Devin.

Quick verdict

You already pay for Devin Pro, Max or Teams: switch the model picker (or /model in Devin CLI) to SWE-2.1 now. In Devin Desktop and Devin CLI it's free through Dec 8 (Pacific time), then still cheap: $0.50/$2.50 per million tokens until Jan 30, 2027. You don't use Devin yet and want a cheap second coding agent: two free weeks of a near-frontier model are worth $20 for one month of Devin Pro. Set a reminder to cancel around Dec 8 if it doesn't stick. You want the strongest coder regardless of cost: Cognition's own charts still put Claude Opus 5.5 ahead on every coding benchmark it published, and GPT-6.2 Sol and Gemini 4 Neon ahead on most. Claude Pro ($20) with Claude Code remains our default pick. You live in GitHub Copilot: MAI Code 2 is the closest price match ($0.50/$2.50 promo until May 17, 2027) and needs no new subscription.

Cognition's methodology note: closed models run in their developers' preferred harnesses (Claude Code, Codex, Antigravity CLI and so on), while open and SWE models run in Devin CLI. FrontierCode is Cognition's own benchmark. Note that the base model family, Kimi K3.1, still beats SWE-2.1 on Terminal-Bench 4.1.

The cybersecurity jump is the bigger story. SWE-2.1 scores 80.0 on ExploitBench (SWE-2: 36.1; Opus 5.5: 91; GPT-6.2 Sol: 100) and 27.3% on ExploitGym (SWE-2: 12.1; Opus 5.5: 34.5). Cognition says the model found new holes in its own training sandbox four times during training, and it ships with safeguards aimed at "a narrow set of high-harm activities." Early users say those safeguards sometimes block routine requests.
